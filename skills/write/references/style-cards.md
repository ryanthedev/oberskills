# Style Cards

Registers and house styles anyone can pick. Load this when the ask names a style ("write like The Economist", "AP style", "plain English", "GOV.UK style", "our house style") or a direction ("punchier", "more formal", "warmer", "drier"). Every card is distilled from a published guide and paraphrased; consult the guide itself for mechanical detail (numerals, capitalization, citation form) rather than guessing.

Sources: *The Economist Style Guide*; *The Associated Press Stylebook*; GOV.UK, *Writing to GOV.UK standards* and the A to Z style guide (guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk, checked 2026-09-21); Mailchimp, *Content Style Guide*, Voice and Tone (styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone, checked 2026-09-21); *The Chicago Manual of Style*; the *Federal Plain Language Guidelines* (plainlanguage.gov was retired and now redirects to the plain language guides at digital.gov/guides/plain-language, checked 2026-09-21; the law behind them is the Plain Writing Act of 2010); George Orwell, "Politics and the English Language" (1946); William Zinsser, *On Writing Well*; Joseph M. Williams, *Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace*.

## 1. How a card meets the core rules

The conflict is real. Plain-language and wire-service styles want uniformity: short sentences, one register, the most-needed thing first. *Lurch*, *Spike*, and *Wander* want variance. A piece can't fully obey both.

The rule: **a card's explicit rules win where they speak. The core rules govern the latitude left over. Each card says which core rules it narrows.**

"Narrows" means the range shrinks, not that the rule switches off. Under a plain-language card the very long sentence is gone, but a fragment next to a full sentence is still available, and still needed: a page of same-length sentences is compliant and reads as machine output. Spend the variance the card leaves you. Where a card is silent, the core rules apply in full.

The surface kill-list survives every card. No published guide asks for "delve".

## 2. The cards

| Card | Signature rules | Narrows | Leaves in full |
|------|-----------------|---------|----------------|
| **The Economist** | Plain words over grand ones; short over long. Cut jargon and journalese. Opinionated, with the argument carried by fact. Wit is dry and rationed. An institutional voice: no "I". Takes Orwell's six rules as its starting point. | Shift Register: the range runs urbane to wry, never chatty. No first-person asides. | Lurch, Spike, Get Specific. Wander inside whatever shape the piece has. |
| **AP** | News register. Inverted pyramid, short paragraphs. The writer has no opinions; every claim is attributed, and the attribution verb is "said". Neutral, consistent mechanics. | Wander: the pyramid owns the order. Shift Register: one neutral register, and the "have an opinion" clause is off. The bland-verb upgrade in the surface rules: "said" stays "said". | Get Specific (it's the whole job). Lurch within short paragraphs. |
| **GOV.UK** | Write for the user need. Plain English; front-load titles, summaries, and sentences. Active voice. Address the user as "you". A published list of words to avoid. Clear over clever, always. | Lurch: the long end is cut off. Spike: uniformly low density is the goal. Wander: most-needed first. Shift Register: one register. | Get Specific. Short-end variance: fragments, questions as headings, a two-word sentence. |
| **Plain language** (US federal tradition) | Know the audience and write in words they use. Topic sentences. Active voice. Tell readers up front what to expect. Tables and lists for detail. | Same as GOV.UK. List Addiction in `references/deep-craft.md` relaxes: lists are a recommended tool here. | Get Specific. Short-end variance. |
| **Mailchimp** | Plainspoken, genuine, a translator of jargon, dry humor. Voice stays constant; tone moves with the reader's state of mind. Clear beats entertaining, and forced humor is worse than none. | Shift Register: bounded by how the reader feels. No jokes at someone who is frustrated or has just lost data. | Everything else. |
| **Chicago** | The formal book and scholarly register. Consistent mechanics, full citation apparatus, serial comma. | Surface rule on contractions: relaxed for formal work. Shift Register: a narrower, more formal band. The one-em-dash cap stays; Chicago permits the mark and doesn't require it. | Lurch, Spike, Wander, Get Specific. Formal is not uniform. |
| **Orwell** | Six rules: no figure of speech you're used to seeing in print; short words over long; cut every word that can go; active over passive; everyday English over foreign, scientific, or jargon words; break any of these before writing something barbarous. | Nothing. | All five. The sixth rule is the license for them. |
| **Zinsser** | Clutter is the disease. Strip every sentence to its cleanest parts. Write as a person to a person: first person, warmth, and humanity are assets. Rewriting is the work. | Nothing. | All five. |
| **Williams** | Characters as subjects, actions as verbs. Old information before new. The stress falls at the end of the sentence. Cohesion from sentence to sentence. | Syntactic Variety in `references/deep-craft.md`: its nominalization and inversion moves ("Testing happened overnight") break Williams. Vary by length, clause depth, questions, and fragments instead. | Lurch, Spike, Wander, Get Specific. |

An organization's documented house voice is a style card. When the user supplies or points to their guide, read it and treat it as one more row: explicit rules win, note what it narrows, core rules take the rest.

## 3. Register dials

For asks that name a direction rather than a target. Each dial is a set of moves, with the counterfeit to avoid. A dial is a line-edit license (`references/edit-levels.md`): it changes sentences, not structure.

| Dial | Moves | Not this |
|------|-------|----------|
| **Punchier** | Cut qualifiers and warm-up clauses. Put the strong word at the end of the sentence. Shorten the sentences that carry claims; leave the long ones for evidence. Verbs over adjectives. | Exclamation marks, hype words, or one-sentence paragraphs all the way down. |
| **More formal** | Full forms for contractions where the genre demands it. Exact terms over idiom. Cut asides. Turn hedges into stated conditions ("if the migration completes" for "hopefully"). | Longer words, passives, "utilize". Formality is distance, not inflation. |
| **Warmer** | Second person. Name the reader's situation before giving the information. Contractions. Concede what's hard. | Exclamation marks, emoji, or anything in the Sycophancy table of `references/surface-rules.md`. |
| **Drier** | Understatement. Delete intensifiers and emotional adjectives and let the facts carry the feeling. Any humor is deadpan. | Bloodless passives. Dry still has an actor in every sentence. |
| **Simpler** | One idea per sentence. The common word. Define a term once or replace it. Known before unknown. | Talking down. Over-Explanation in `references/deep-craft.md` still applies. |

Two dials can run together ("warmer and punchier"). Opposed dials ("more formal and warmer") aren't a contradiction: formal in construction, warm in what gets acknowledged.

## 4. Style by extraction

The fallback for any target without a published guide: "write like this post", "match our blog", "like Hunter S. Thompson".

Build a disposable card from samples. Best case, the user supplies one to sound like and one to avoid; the contrast shows which traits matter. Use the extraction method in `voices/_template.md` (patterns, not paraphrase; lexicon, sentence mechanics, how the writer reasons, an anti-style list, a short verbatim sample bank) and keep only what's needed for this piece. Then apply it like any card: say what it narrows, and let the core rules take the rest.

When the target is a well-known writer and no sample is supplied, work only from traits you can name with confidence, and keep the card short. A wrong trait produces caricature. Aim for the register, not a pastiche, and don't reproduce the writer's passages. When the piece matters and the result would hinge on a guess, a supplied sample beats a longer card.

## 5. Style is not Voice

A **voice profile** is one specific person. It's private, it persists across pieces, and it's judged by "would they actually send this?". A **style card** is a register anyone can pick. It lives for the piece, and it's judged by whether a reader of that publication or genre would find it at home.

They stack. A voice profile can write under a card (the founder's voice, in the company's house style): the card's explicit rules win, and the voice fills the latitude, the same way the core rules do.
